# BUG-005 — Customer feedback publicly shows the author's email with only the first 3 characters hidden

**Reported by:** Carina
**Date:** 2026-11-10
**Component:** Customer Feedback form (☰ → Customer Feedback) and About Us page — POST /api/Feedbacks
**Environment:** OWASP Juice Shop 20.2.0 (Docker), http://localhost:3000, Chrome
**Severity:** Major
**Priority:** High

## Preconditions
- A registered account, logged in.

## Steps to reproduce
1. Log in (e.g. as `test@test.com`).
2. Open ☰ → Customer Feedback.
3. Comment: `Double Comment`. Choose any rating, answer the CAPTCHA.
4. Open DevTools → Network, clear it, click Submit.
5. Click the POST Feedbacks request → Payload, and look at `comment`.
6. Account → Logout.
7. Open ☰ → About Us and wait for the customer feedback to show the comment.

## Expected result
Feedback is published without the author's email, or the user is clearly told
before submitting that their email will be shown. The app does not add personal
data to the comment without the user knowing.

## Actual result
- The app adds the author's email to the comment before sending it. I typed only
  `Double Comment`; the Payload shows
  `comment: "Double Comment (***t1011@test.com)"`.
- About Us shows the comment with that email, with only the first 3 characters
  replaced by `***`.
- It is visible while logged out, so anyone visiting the site can see it.

## Reproducibility
3 of 3, with three accounts:

| Account | Shown on About Us |
|---|---|
| `test1011@test.com` | `***t1011@test.com` |
| `test@test.com` | `***t@test.com` |
| `tst@test.com` | `***@test.com` |

## Notes / impact
- The frontend builds the author as `***` + the email minus its first 3 characters
  (code: `***${email.slice(3)}`). Feedback sent while logged out shows `anonymous`.
- So how much of an email is hidden depends on its length: a 3-character name
  (`tst@`) is fully hidden, but a real email like `maria.popescu@gmail.com` would
  show as `***ia.popescu@gmail.com` and still identify the person.
- An email address is personal data. Publishing it on a public page without
  telling the user is a GDPR concern for EU users.
- Severity Major because personal data is exposed publicly to anyone, not just
  shown in an inconvenient way. Compare BUG-004 (Minor), where nothing is exposed.

**Oracle:** Standards & statutes (GDPR: an email address is personal data, published
without notice) and User expectations (people leaving feedback don't expect their
email to appear next to it).
